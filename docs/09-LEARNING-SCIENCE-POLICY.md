# Learning Science Policy

## Status of evidence

This policy separates research-supported design directions from product values that require calibration.

Research-supported directions:

- Active retrieval and distributed practice support longer retention better than exposure alone.
- Interaction combines input, output, negotiation of meaning, and opportunities for repair.
- Corrective feedback can improve second-language learning, but timing and form affect results.
- Motivation is better supported when autonomy, competence, and relatedness are considered together.
- CEFR-aligned communication includes reception, production, interaction, mediation, and communication strategies.

Product hypotheses requiring pilot evidence:

- First-pass accuracy window of 0.60 to 0.90.
- Two consecutive high-success attempts before support fades.
- Initial review intervals of 10 minutes, 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 14 days, and 30 days.
- Maximum of two feedback focus points.
- Lesson pass thresholds of 70 or 75.

## Input and cognitive load

1. Introduce language inside a meaningful scene or task.
2. Test first-pass comprehension before revealing the full transcript or explanation.
3. Offer supports in an explicit sequence such as image, slower replay, chunk model, transcript, and Thai explanation.
4. Fade one support at a time only after repeated success.
5. Do not add undeclared vocabulary or grammar inside an assessed task.
6. If performance stays below the lower pilot bound, reduce task complexity before increasing repetition.

## Retrieval and spacing

1. End initial learning with recall without the model.
2. Schedule a short first retrieval and expand the interval after successful retrieval.
3. Reset after failed retrieval and record the lapse rather than treating completion as mastery.
4. Change wording, speaker, audience, or situation on at least one review.
5. Mix due prior competencies with the current unit, but keep each review target explicit.
6. Version the scheduler so later calibration does not reinterpret historical progress.

## Interaction and mediation

- Every unit includes a goal-oriented interaction with clarification or repair.
- B1-C1 units include mediation such as relaying information, summarizing, explaining data, or adapting language for an audience.
- Roleplay success depends on communicative outcome, not merely grammatical form.
- The learner must sometimes respond without notes or a visible model.

## Feedback

- Do not interrupt fluency turns for minor errors.
- Prioritize task failure, then intelligibility, target language, and minor accuracy.
- Present no more than two focus points per retry under the pilot policy.
- Use observation, reason, improved model, and learner retry.
- Preserve learner facts and intended meaning; never invent career achievements.
- Track whether feedback was viewed, understood, retried, and retained later.

## Motivation and self-regulation

- Autonomy: offer two to five personally meaningful choices rather than unlimited configuration.
- Competence: show concrete can-do evidence and progress from supported to independent performance.
- Relatedness: offer safe interaction with AI and optional practice with a trusted person, peer, or mentor.
- Reflection: ask what strategy worked or what evidence improved, then use the response to guide the next recommendation.
- Avoid public leaderboards and punitive streak loss during the pilot.

## Calibration rule

No numerical threshold, scheduler interval, or automated open-response score becomes `calibrated` from developer judgment alone. Calibration requires learner data, independent human ratings for open responses, delayed transfer evidence, subgroup review, and a documented decision record.
