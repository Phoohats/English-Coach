# A1-C1 Curriculum Map

## 1. Progression model

The curriculum uses CEFR as a design reference and reports product-specific mastery. It does not claim to grant an official CEFR qualification.

Progress is represented as a competency graph. A learner can be strong in reading at B2 while speaking interaction remains at B1. Recommendations must therefore use skill and objective evidence rather than one global level alone.

## 2. Competency families

| Family | What is measured |
|---|---|
| Listening | gist, detail, inference, speaker intent, accent tolerance |
| Speaking | intelligibility, fluency, range, accuracy, interaction, repair |
| Reading | gist, detail, inference, structure, source purpose |
| Writing | task completion, organization, range, accuracy, register |
| Language resources | vocabulary, grammar, chunks, discourse markers |
| Mediation | relaying information, summarizing, explaining data, adapting language for an audience |
| Learning strategy | planning, monitoring, repair, retrieval, reflection, and support fading |
| Career communication | evidence, clarity, stakeholder fit, persuasion, professional register |

## 3. Level outcomes and domains

| Level | Product outcome | Core domains |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Exchange basic personal and everyday information with support | identity, family, time, routines, food, places, shopping, asking for help |
| A2 | Complete predictable everyday tasks and short social exchanges | travel, home, health, services, plans, past events, messages, workplace basics |
| B1 | Maintain connected communication about familiar life and work | experiences, opinions, problems, projects, email, interviews, teamwork, storytelling |
| B2 | Participate independently in professional and abstract discussion | meetings, presentations, negotiation, technical explanation, behavioral interviews, networking |
| C1 | Communicate flexibly and precisely in complex professional contexts | leadership, persuasion, strategy, conflict, crisis, stakeholder communication, advanced interviews |

The table is a course-level target. Individual lessons may vary by up to 15 percentage points when the unit restores overall balance.

## 5. Full roadmap size

The planning baseline is eight units per level and six lessons per unit, resulting in 48 lessons per level and 240 lessons across A1-C1. This is a roadmap capacity, not the launch commitment.

Each six-lesson unit follows this pattern:

1. Comprehensible input and vocabulary in context.
2. Listening discrimination, meaning discovery, and first retrieval.
3. Guided speaking and pronunciation for intelligibility with selective feedback.
4. Reading-to-writing transfer or mediation for a defined audience.
5. Interactive scenario with clarification and repair strategies.
6. Integrated transfer task, delayed review scheduling, learner choice, and reflection.

## 6. Pilot vertical slices

### A1 slice: 12 lessons

- Unit 1: Meeting people.
- Unit 2: Daily routines and time.
- Integrated task: meet a new colleague and arrange a simple time.

### Career slice: 12 lessons

- Unit 1: Professional introduction and career story, primarily B1.
- Unit 2: Behavioral interview and follow-up questions, primarily B1-B2.
- Integrated task: complete a ten-minute mock interview and improve two answers.

## 7. Promotion evidence

A learner advances when all conditions are met:

- Core objectives reach the configured mastery threshold.
- The learner succeeds on a delayed review after at least one interval.
- The integrated task meets its rubric threshold.
- At least two independent samples support the decision.
- One sample changes the wording, speaker, audience, or situation to test transfer.
- No prerequisite competency remains below its minimum.
- Open responses have enough evidence to avoid a false pass from a single short answer.

Level labels remain estimates until the full placement and calibration study is completed.

The initial pass thresholds are product hypotheses. Calibration uses human-rated independent performances and delayed transfer outcomes; completing a lesson cannot by itself establish a CEFR level.

## 8. Difficulty controls

| Control | A1 | B1 | C1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thai support | available by default | optional explanations | on request only |
| Speech support | model, chunks, retry | outline then free response | unprepared response and interruption |
| Listening | slow and natural versions | mostly natural speed | natural speed, varied speakers and implicit meaning |
| Writing | words and short messages | connected paragraphs | precise, audience-aware professional text |
| AI freedom | tightly bounded choices | bounded follow-ups | broad scenario within explicit rubric |

## 9. Learning psychology controls

- Difficulty: add support below the provisional first-pass window, maintain challenge inside it, and fade support only after repeated success.
- Retrieval: recall without the model, then revisit the same competency with changed surface form and increasing intervals.
- Feedback: prioritize task completion and intelligibility, limit focus to two points, and require an immediate retry.
- Fluency: do not interrupt a speaking turn for minor accuracy corrections.
- Autonomy: offer a small set of personally meaningful scenario, role, or topic choices.
- Competence: show progress as a concrete `can-do` performance rather than points alone.
- Relatedness: include an AI role plus an optional trusted-person, peer, or mentor extension where appropriate.
- Cognitive load: reveal transcript, Thai explanation, model, and chunks progressively; fade them after demonstrated success.
- Interleaving: due review may mix earlier competencies, but new instruction introduces only the declared language scope.
- Reflection: ask the learner to identify a useful strategy or evidence of improvement, not merely rate enjoyment.

## 10. Content quality checklist

- Language matches the declared CEFR target and prerequisite vocabulary.
- Scenario has a plausible communicative purpose.
- Images do not reveal the answer accidentally or create cultural ambiguity.
- Audio includes transcript, speaker metadata, and approved pronunciation target.
- Instructions remain understandable without a teacher.
- Distractors are plausible but unambiguously incorrect.
- Feedback explains meaning before terminology.
- Every open task includes examples at below, meeting, and exceeding expectations.
- Review variants preserve the target competency while changing context or wording.
- Feedback does not exceed the lesson's focus-point limit.
- Mediation tasks declare source, audience, and information that must be preserved.
