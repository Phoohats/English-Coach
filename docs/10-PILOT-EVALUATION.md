# Pilot Learning Evaluation

## Purpose

The pilot tests whether learners retain and transfer the target competencies. It does not certify CEFR levels or attempt to prove the entire A1-C1 curriculum from two units.

## Stages

### Stage 1: usability and instrumentation

- Recruit a small A1 group and a small B1-B2 career group.
- Verify instructions, recording flow, feedback comprehension, session length, and review notifications.
- Confirm that all attempts pin content, rubric, scheduler, prompt, and model versions.
- Use results to estimate variance and conduct a power analysis for the calibration study rather than inventing a universal sample size.

### Stage 2: learning signal

- Pre-test each target with an unfamiliar prompt.
- Complete the instructional lesson.
- Immediate post-test without the model.
- Delayed retrieval after the configured intervals.
- Delayed transfer task with changed wording, speaker, audience, or situation.

### Stage 3: calibration

- Two trained human raters independently score sampled speaking and writing responses.
- Resolve rubric ambiguity before using automated scores for promotion.
- Model the relationship between lesson scores and delayed transfer success.
- Choose or revise thresholds from evidence and document uncertainty by level and activity family.
- Review performance by relevant learner groups and device/audio conditions to detect unfair failure patterns.

## Measures

| Dimension | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Learning | pre, immediate, delayed, and transfer performance |
| Retention | retrieval success and lapse pattern over time |
| Transfer | success with changed context, wording, or audience |
| Feedback | viewed, understood, retried, corrected, retained |
| Motivation | meaningful choice use, perceived competence, willingness to continue, optional relatedness use |
| Cognitive load | support requests, repeated instruction views, abandonment point, learner report |
| Fairness | human-versus-automated disagreement and subgroup error patterns |
| Efficiency | learning gain relative to active time and AI cost |

## GO criteria

- No P1 privacy, data-loss, or cross-user finding.
- Learners can complete the critical path without facilitator rescue.
- Delayed performance remains above pre-test performance for the target competency.
- At least one changed-context task shows transfer rather than memorized repetition.
- Human raters can apply the rubric consistently enough to support calibration.
- Automated feedback preserves learner facts and does not systematically penalize intelligible Thai-accented speech.
- Review scheduling and feedback load do not create unacceptable abandonment or anxiety signals.

Exact numeric release thresholds will be set after Stage 1 exposes baseline rates. They must be versioned and justified in the pilot report.

## NO-GO conditions

- Immediate scores rise but delayed or transfer performance does not.
- Threshold decisions rely on lesson completion alone.
- Automated scoring disagrees materially with human judgment without a known correction path.
- Feedback invents facts, overwhelms the learner, or repeatedly targets accent similarity instead of intelligibility.
- Any learner cannot delete voice, transcript, CV, or account data as promised.
